On The Call

Cold Call Guide

You have about 8 seconds before someone decides to keep listening or hang up. The goal of a cold call is one thing: earn a conversation, not make a sale.

⚡ Next Best Action
1
The opening — earn 30 more seconds
0:00 to 0:15

Use their name, say who you are, and get to the point. No one wants small talk from a cold caller.

"Hey [Name], this is [Your name] from AgentNav. I'll be quick. We built a platform for health insurance brokers — quoting for ACA, STM, Life, Ancillary, and Indemnity, a full CRM, SMS, and a tool that runs your entire Open Enrollment season without you doing it manually. Does that sound like something worth two minutes?"

The question at the end matters. It gives them control and respects their time.

2
If they say sure, tell me more
0:15 to 1:30

Ask before you talk. The best thing you can do after they give you a minute is use it to learn about them.

"Really quick before I pitch you anything. How are you currently managing renewals when OE hits? Like specifically, what does that process look like for your book?"

This is your most important question on a cold call. Their answer tells you exactly which pain point to hit.

Listen for the words: manually, spreadsheet, calling everyone myself, some clients fall through, overwhelmed. Any of these is your entry point.

3
Connect their pain to one specific thing AgentNav does
1:30 to 2:30

Don't list features. Respond to what they just told you.

"That's exactly what Renewal Autopilot is built for. It sends the outreach to your clients automatically, tracks who responds, generates updated quotes for ACA, STM, Life, and Ancillary products, and gives you a board where every client has a status. You're not chasing 200 people in November. You're just reviewing what the system surfaced for you."

One feature. One pain. One connection. Don't add more.

4
Ask for the meeting
2:30 to 3:00

Don't ask if they want to learn more. Ask for a specific time.

"I'd love to show you how this looks for a book your size. Do you have 20 minutes this week or next — Thursday afternoon or Friday morning?"

Two specific options. Not "are you free sometime?" Give them a choice, not an open invitation to say no.

5
If they say no or not now
Any point

"Totally fair. When does OE stress usually start hitting for your team? I'll follow up right before that so the timing actually makes sense."

Never argue. Plant a seed and get permission to come back at the right moment.

On The Call

Discovery Call Guide

Discovery is the most important call in the sales process. If you do this right, the demo writes itself and the close is easy. The goal is to understand their world before you say anything about ours.

The golden rule of discovery

You should be talking about 30 percent of the time. They should be talking 70 percent. If you're the one doing most of the talking, you've already lost the call.

1
Set the agenda and earn trust early
0:00 to 3:00

"Thanks for making time. Here's what I was hoping to do today. I want to spend most of this call learning about how you're set up right now, what's working and what isn't. Then if I think AgentNav could actually help, I'll be honest about that. And if it's not a fit I'll tell you that too. Does that work for you?"

This sets expectations, positions you as a consultant not a salesperson, and makes them comfortable being honest with you.

2
Understand their current setup
3:00 to 12:00

Work through these questions naturally. Don't read them like a checklist.

  • 1
    How big is your agency right now, and is it just you or do you have a team?
  • 2
    What lines of business are you mostly working in? Is it mostly ACA or do you also do STM, Life, Ancillary, Indemnity, or Medicare?
  • 3
    Walk me through what your tech stack looks like today. What tools are you using?
  • 4
    When a new lead comes in, what happens? Where does it go?
  • 5
    How do you handle quoting right now? Are you using a comparative rater or going carrier by carrier?
  • 6
    What does a typical client conversation look like from your side? How do you track what was said?
  • 7
    How do you manage renewals? Walk me through what OE looks like for your team.
  • 8
    What tools are you using for SMS or client communication?
3
Find the real pain
12:00 to 20:00

Once you've heard their setup, go deeper on what's breaking. These are the follow-up questions that uncover real buying motivation.

  • 1
    What's the most frustrating part of your current setup right now?
  • 2
    Last OE — how many clients did you lose track of or lose entirely?
  • 3
    If you could change one thing about how your team works today, what would it be?
  • 4
    What would make next OE feel different from last year?

"It sounds like the thing that's really costing you time right now is [their exact words]. Is that fair to say? Because that's actually one of the specific things AgentNav was built to fix."

Use their exact words back to them. Don't paraphrase. It shows you listened.

4
Set up the demo
20:00 to 25:00

"Based on what you told me, I want to show you three specific things in the demo — the client record because it sounds like tracking conversations is a problem, Renewal Autopilot because OE is clearly where the pain is, and the quoting flow because going carrier by carrier is eating your time. Does that sound like the right focus?"

Name exactly what you'll show and why. This makes the demo feel tailored, not canned.

On The Call

Demo Call Guide

A good demo is not a feature tour. It's a story. The client is the main character. AgentNav is what makes their story go better.

Before you start sharing your screen

Spend the first three minutes confirming what you learned in discovery. "Last time we talked you mentioned X was the biggest frustration. Is that still the right thing to focus on today?" This shows you listened and keeps the demo focused on what actually matters to them.

1
Start with the client record, not the home screen
0:00 to 8:00

Open a client profile and walk through it like you're looking at a real person. The Rx and medical profile, household members, the FPL, the activity log, the task list. Show them this is not a contact database. It's a complete picture of a human being.

"This is what your client looks like inside AgentNav. Every piece of information you need before a call is right here. Medical profile, income, family members, what was said last time, what needs to happen next. You never have to dig through emails or notes apps before picking up the phone."

2
Show the quoting flow as a continuous story
8:00 to 18:00

Don't just show the quoting tool. Show how the client data flows in automatically so they never re-enter anything. Build an A, B, C proposal. Show the side-by-side comparison. Show supplemental coverage — STM, Life, Ancillary, Indemnity — getting layered in.

"Everything you entered in the client profile came with us. No re-entry. Now I'm building three options side by side. Bronze, Silver, and Gold. I can add dental on Option B and a life plan on Option C. When I share this proposal, the client sees exactly what you want them to see."

3
Show Renewal Autopilot like it's the main event
18:00 to 30:00

This is where most demos win or lose. Don't rush past it. Take your time and let them see the full board.

"This is Renewal Autopilot. Every one of your renewal clients sits on this board with a status. Not Started. Outreach Sent. Responded. Meeting Scheduled. Re-Enrolled. The system sends the outreach automatically. It tracks who responds. It pulls updated quotes. You're not chasing 200 people in November. You're just reviewing what landed in your queue."

Let them react. Ask: "How does your team handle this today?" Get them talking about the gap before you move on.

4
For small agencies — show team visibility
30:00 to 38:00

If they have more than one agent, show the team view. This is often where the demo closes itself.

"Right now you have no idea what your agents are doing without asking them. Here, you can see every agent's pipeline, what they've quoted, what's pending enrollment, and which clients haven't been touched. You don't have to manage by asking. You manage by looking."

Note: For a solo agent running a combined demo and closing call, skip team visibility and go straight to the close.

5
End the demo with a question, not a pitch
Last 5 min

"That's the core of what AgentNav does. Based on what you told me earlier about [their specific pain], does this feel like it solves that? What did you see today that felt most relevant to your situation?"

Get them talking. Their answer tells you exactly how to close.

On The Call

Closing Call Guide

If you've done discovery and demo well, the closing call should feel like a natural next step, not a pressure moment. Most closes happen before the closing call starts.

1
Start by confirming where they are
0:00 to 5:00

"Since we last spoke, I put together the right plan for your team. Before I walk you through it, I just want to check: has anything changed on your end? Still seeing the same challenges you mentioned with OE and the renewal workflow?"

If they say yes, you're on track. If something has changed, you want to know before you pitch.

2
Present a recommendation, not a menu
5:00 to 12:00

Don't show three plans and let them pick. Tell them which one is right for them and why, based on what they told you.

"Based on what you shared, you have four agents and an assistant doing most of the client communication. That's exactly what the Small Agency plan at $247 a month is built for. It's about $49 per person, everyone gets their own SMS line and full access, and you get white label branding so the whole thing looks like your agency. That's the one I'd recommend."

3
Ask directly and then go quiet
12:00 to 14:00

"Does that sound like the right fit for where you are right now?"

Ask the question. Then stop talking. Don't fill the silence. Let them respond. The first person to speak after that question usually loses.

4
For solo agents — combine demo and close in one call
Solo agent flow

Solo agents make decisions faster and don't need consensus. After the demo, move directly to the close in the same call. Don't schedule a separate closing call.

"You've seen the platform and you know what it does. For a solo agent your size, it's $79 a month, everything included. I can get you set up today and we can do your onboarding call this week while what you saw is still fresh. Want to get started?"

Solo agents who say "let me think about it" rarely come back. Create gentle urgency by offering to get started immediately.

5
Handle the last objection and lock it down
14:00 to close

There will almost always be one more thing. Address it directly.

"That's fair. Here's what I'd suggest — let's get you started on a month-to-month basis. No annual contract, no risk. If it's not working after 30 days, you're out. But based on everything you told me, I think you'll know it's right within the first week."

On The Call

Onboarding Call Guide

The onboarding call is when someone goes from "I bought this" to "I believe in this." First impressions of the platform stick. Make them count.

The goal of onboarding

Get the new user to one real win in the first 20 minutes. Not a tour of every feature. One thing that makes them say "this is actually going to save me time." For most agents, that's either the client profile or the Renewal Autopilot board.

1
Celebrate the decision and set the agenda
0:00 to 3:00

"Welcome to AgentNav. I'm really glad you're here. Today we're going to focus on getting you set up in a way that makes sense for your book, not just clicking through every screen. What I want is for you to end this call feeling like you could use it on your own tomorrow. Sound good?"

2
Import their data together
3:00 to 15:00

Don't tell them how to do the policy import. Do it with them. Have them screen share and walk through it together. When they see their real clients show up in the platform, the product becomes real.

"Let's get your clients in here right now. If you have a spreadsheet or can export from wherever you're working today, we'll walk through the import together. Nothing makes the platform feel more real than seeing your actual book in it."

3
Configure their SMS line and calendar
15:00 to 25:00

Get their dedicated SMS number assigned. Connect their Google Calendar. These two things make the platform feel like it belongs to them, not like borrowed software.

"You now have a dedicated SMS number inside AgentNav. Every text you send from here is logged to the client record automatically. Your clients will save this number. It becomes your professional line."

4
Run Renewal Autopilot setup together
25:00 to 40:00

Walk through the Autopilot board. Show them how to activate it for their renewal clients. Let them turn on the first client themselves.

"Pick one client from your book — someone you know is up for renewal this year. Turn on Autopilot for them right now. The system will send the outreach, track the response, and pull an updated quote automatically. You just watched the OE workflow start for real."

Letting them do it themselves is the difference between a tutorial and a transformation.

5
End with next steps, not homework
40:00 to 45:00

"Before we hang up, I want you to do one thing this week: add five more clients to Autopilot. Not all of them — just five. That's it. And if anything comes up, here's how to reach me directly."

One small action beats a long to-do list. They'll come back with momentum instead of overwhelm.

When They Ask What AgentNav Does

The short version

AgentNav is everything a health insurance broker needs in one place. Most agents are running four or five different tools that don't talk to each other. We put the CRM, quoting for ACA, STM, Life, Ancillary, and Indemnity products, SMS, scheduling, policy management, marketing campaigns, and the entire Open Enrollment workflow into one platform. It starts at $79 a month for a solo agent and scales to teams from there.

After A Competitor Comparison

The positioning statement

Most of the platforms out there were either built for P&C agents and adapted for health, or built for group benefits and not really designed for individual ACA. AgentNav was built from the ground up for agents selling ACA health plans — and the full suite of ancillary products around them. That means things like the medical and Rx profile in the client record, the FPL tracking, the side-by-side ACA, STM, Life, and Indemnity proposal builder, and the Renewal Autopilot that manages Open Enrollment automatically. Those aren't add-ons. They're the product.

When Renewal Autopilot Comes Up

The OE workflow pitch

Renewal Autopilot is the thing I'd want you to walk away remembering. It's a board where every single one of your renewal clients sits with a clear status. Not Started, Follow-Up Needed, Meeting Scheduled, Re-Enrollment, Completed. For each client with Autopilot turned on, the system handles the outreach automatically. It sends the campaign, waits for the client to fill out their preferences form, and then generates a new quote based on what they come back with. You review and approve. You're not chasing 200 people in November. You're just reviewing what the system surfaced for you.

When Price Comes Up vs. A Cheaper Tool

The real cost question

What does your current stack cost? Add up your CRM, your quoting tool, your texting app, your enrollment platform, and whatever you're using for policy tracking. Most agents are at $200 to $400 a month when they add it all up — and still don't have everything integrated. AgentNav replaces all of it for $79 a month for a solo agent. But forget the price comparison for a second. How many clients did you lose in the last OEP because someone fell through a crack? That's the real cost number that matters.

When They Say "We're Happy With What We Have"

The comfort trap

I hear that a lot. And usually it's true — until November hits. What does your OEP process look like today? Walk me through it. [Let them answer.] Okay — how many hours does that take, and how many clients fall through? Most agents who say they're happy with what they have are still spending three or four weeks manually working renewals every year. If you could cut that to a few days with Renewal Autopilot already configured, would that be worth 20 minutes of your time?

When They Ask About Team Features

The agency visibility pitch

Right now, the only way you know what your agents are doing is by asking them. AgentNav changes that. You can see every agent's pipeline, what they've quoted, what's pending enrollment, who hasn't followed up in a week, and which clients are sitting in the renewal queue with no action. You don't manage by asking. You manage by looking. And every agent has their own SMS number, their own client records, their own Renewal Autopilot board — but you see all of it from the top.
